Reading of the period

The economics register is what actually scales. The notes that broke 100 are overwhelmingly the ones that read as material grievance with a number attached — wage stagnation, the mortgage double standard, the 1994 unemployment-metric change, boomer-vs-Gen-Z time horizons. These travel outside the follower base because a reader does not have to share the surrounding worldview to agree with them. The explicitly ideological notes, by contrast, cluster tightly in the 5–25 range: they are read by the same forty accounts every time.

GLO knows this and says so out loud. An unusual share of the period (~15 notes) is meta-commentary on Substack itself, including a direct statement of the tradeoff: stating an ideological position openly scares off the "edgy suburban dads" and roughly halves engagement. So the account is being run with visible awareness that its highest-reach material and its actual convictions are not the same thing, and it keeps choosing both.

The main opponent this period was the mainstream right, not the left. Peterson, Shapiro, Kirk, Bet-David and Fuentes take more direct fire than any liberal target, framed as controlled opposition that exists to absorb and waste attention. Repeated "I called the Charlie Kirk psyop immediately" claims are used as a credibility ledger against them.

Genuinely new since May: the EU surveillance cluster (Chat Control 1.0, Flock cameras, digital ID) with its sealed-envelope argument, an AI thread that is skeptical-instrumental rather than doom-laden (Kissinger/Schmidt on perception management, vibe-coding dismissal) while GLO openly uses Claude to translate obscure texts, and a small but persistent occult-reading lane. The exit thesis — remote income, leave, Mexico as the inflation hedge — is the one recurring piece of advice rather than diagnosis.
